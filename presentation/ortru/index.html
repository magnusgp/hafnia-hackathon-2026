The problem

One AI assessment can say no
to something that matters.

Hafnia“…no descent onto the tracks or movement onto the railway track area is visible.”The original answer, kept on record: No entry observed.
This is the real case that started the project. A person sits on the platform edge and about four seconds later climbs down onto the tracks. Hafnia read the footage and answered that no entry onto the track area was visible. That answer is a model's assessment, not a fact, and an operator who only saw that answer would move on. We keep Hafnia's answer on record; the point is that it should not be the last word.

The second watcher

An independent detector kept its own record
of the same footage.

Hafnia
No entry observed (one answer for the whole recording)
Local vision
person at the region from 11:03:44.5

A person detector on a Linux A10G placed the person at the platform edge about four seconds before the climb. That is proximity, not entry. Where the two disagree, there is something worth a human look.

Independent vision is a different kind of observer. A detector on the Linux GPU measures where tracked people are, twice a second, against a region. It places the person at the platform edge from 11:03:44.5. It does not claim entry; it only claims proximity. Hafnia says no, the detector says there is a person at the hazard. That is not a contradiction. They are two observers. Ortru keeps them apart and puts them on one timeline.

What Ortru does

Ortru connects both to XProtect
and applies a policy you define.

  1. XProtectrecords the camera
  2. Hafniaanswers the Watch's questionLocal visionmeasures the region
  3. Watch policyraises an exception when the two call for a look
  4. Operatorinvestigates, decides, confirms
  5. XProtect alarmin Smart Client, once
One line per step. XProtect records. For each Watch, Hafnia is asked the Watch's own question about a recorded window, and local vision measures the Watch's region at the same time. A policy the operator chose decides when the two signals raise an exception. The operator investigates, decides, and only then does a confirmed event reach XProtect, where the operator already works.

The Watch

Point a guardian at a camera.
No code.

  1. Camera from the authorized XProtect catalog
  2. Question in plain words, for Hafnia
  3. Local vision from a verified registry
  4. Region drawn on the picture
  5. Policy, cadence, budget
This is how an operator creates a Watch. Choose the camera from the real XProtect catalog. Write what Hafnia should look for; Ortru adds the fixed answer format and shows the exact request. Pick what local vision watches from a registry of capabilities that actually run; anything unsupported is unavailable and says why. Draw the region on the camera picture. Choose when to raise an exception, how often to look, and a daily Hafnia budget. Save and activate. Every edit is a new version, so completed assessments keep exactly the prompt and settings they used.

Configurable, not hardcoded

Another camera. Its own question,
its own region, its own cadence.

Two Watches on two real cameras, created through the same screen. The platform Watch asks about entry onto the track area. The school gate Watch asks about someone working a gate lock with a tool, with its own region and its own cadence. The platform camera has a native alarm in XProtect. The gate camera does not, and Ortru says so in the Watch instead of showing a send button that cannot work. Review still works; adding an alarm changes XProtect, so it waits for explicit approval.

Monitoring

Recorded windows, at a cadence,
inside a budget.

  1. 1Check the recording is still retained
  2. 2Read it from XProtect (read only)
  3. 3Run local vision on the A10G
  4. 4Ask Hafnia once, never twice
  5. 5Compare under the Watch policy

Per-Watch and global request limits. A window is assessed once per Watch version. After an uncertain result a request is polled, never resent. This samples recorded windows; it is not streaming.

Monitoring works over recorded windows. At the Watch's cadence Ortru checks the camera still retains the footage, reads the window read-only, runs local vision on the A10G, asks Hafnia exactly once, and compares the two. Budgets limit how often a Watch can call Hafnia. If a result is uncertain the request is polled, never resent. Be plain about it: this samples recorded windows. It is not real-time streaming.

The handoff

Confirm once.
A real event reaches XProtect.

  1. Review decisionYou watched the footage and requested a review
  2. Event ServerAccepted the exact event you confirmed
  3. Smart ClientAlarm verified: right camera, source time, detector box

Three different facts, never merged. Duplicate-send ledger, exact payload fingerprint, no retries. Verified earlier on p022: two events sent once each, both alarms in Smart Client.

The handoff is real and deliberate. The operator reviews the exact event: camera, source time, message, destination and fingerprint, then confirms. It goes through the duplicate-send ledger and is never retried. The Event Server accepting the event and Smart Client showing the alarm are different facts, and Ortru reports them as different states. This was verified earlier on this machine: two events, each sent once, both alarms listed in Smart Client; alarm 5 opens its source recording with the detector box.

Honest limits

Controlled scenarios.
No accuracy claim.

  • Recorded windows, not live streaming
  • Synthetic hackathon scenes, no benchmark, no measured reduction in missed events
  • Hafnia is an AI review; the operator's decision is the human one
  • Native alarms need an operator-approved setup per camera

What is new is a way to stop one assessment from being the last word.

Say the limits out loud. This is controlled scenarios; there is no accuracy benchmark and no production deployment. We process recorded windows, not live video. Hafnia is an AI review and the operator's decision is the human review. New cameras need an operator-approved alarm in XProtect before they can send.
